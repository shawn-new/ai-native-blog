# Against the AI Moratorium: The People's AI Must Be Built
# 反对 AI 暂停键：普通人的 AI 必须被建造出来

## I Agree With The Diagnosis, Not The Prescription
## 我同意诊断，不同意处方

**English:**

I agree with Bernie Sanders's warning about AI power.

Big Tech has every incentive to turn AI into a profit machine: replace labor, suppress wages, expand surveillance, push productivity gains toward capital. Sanders is right that ordinary people should not absorb the costs while billionaires capture the gains.

I reject the remedy. A construction pause does not distribute power. It freezes the current map, and the current map is the thing he objects to.

**Where I am standing.** I build AI products for a living. A slower buildout costs me something, and a faster one pays me something, so treat everything below as an argument from an interested party and check it accordingly. There is a second bias worth naming, because I think it shapes this piece more than the first: I am a development optimist. My prior is that most problems are problems of insufficient development, and that prior is doing real work in what follows. It is also the thing most likely to be wrong. Someone who has watched development arrive and concentrate power anyway would read the same facts differently, and I cannot rule out that they are seeing something I am not.

Safety has to mean more than slowing down. It has to distribute power.

**中文：**

我同意 Bernie Sanders 对 AI 权力结构的警告。

Big Tech 有强烈激励把 AI 变成利润机器：替代劳动，压低工资，扩大监控，把生产率收益集中到资本手里。Sanders 说普通人不该承担成本、让亿万富翁拿走收益，这个判断是对的。

我不同意他的处方。暂停建设不会分散权力。它会把现有格局冻住，而现有格局正是他反对的东西。

**我站在哪里。** 我以做 AI 产品为生。建设放慢让我付出代价，建设加快让我获益，所以下面所有论证请当作一个利益相关方的论证来查。还有第二重偏见更值得说，因为我认为它对这篇文章的影响比第一重更大：我是一个发展乐观主义者。我的先验是，大部分问题都是发展不充足的问题——这个先验在下文里实实在在地起着作用，也是最可能出错的地方。一个见过发展如期到来、权力却照样集中的人，读同样的事实会读出不同结论，我没法排除他看见了我没看见的东西。

安全不该停在减速上。安全要靠权力分散。

## What The Bill Actually Says
## 法案到底写了什么

**English:**

Sanders's video and policy push make one argument: control of AI is concentrating in the hands of a few tech giants and billionaires. Without constraints, AI is more likely to replace labor, suppress wages, generate deepfakes, expand surveillance, and consume local electricity and water.

The policy is the AI Data Center Moratorium Act — S.4214, introduced by Sanders on March 25, 2026, with a House companion, H.R.9442, introduced by Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez on June 24. It is worth reading past the press release, because the provisions are both broader and more conditional than the coverage suggests.

- The trigger is **20 megawatts** of power demand, together with liquid cooling and racks rated at 20kW or above. Twenty megawatts is a mid-sized facility, not only a hyperscaler campus.
- It covers **upgrades to existing data centers**, not just new construction. That is the provision with the most immediate effect, and the one most often left out of summaries — including my own earlier draft of this post.
- The pause lifts when **"strong national safeguards" are in place**. There is no sunset date, no numeric threshold, and no named agency that certifies the condition has been met.
- Projects must show they will **not raise consumer utility bills** and must obtain **local approval**. There are **union labor and prevailing wage requirements**, and a **ban on government subsidies** for data center construction or operation.
- A separate title bans **US exports of AI computing infrastructure** to countries without comparable safeguards.

Several of these I support outright. True-cost utility rates, community approval, and prevailing wage are good policy whether or not anything is paused, and I would vote for them as standalone bills. My objection is narrower than "this bill is bad," and it is aimed at one design choice: an open-ended pause with no defined lift condition.

**中文：**

Sanders 的视频和政策动作说的是同一件事：AI 的控制权正在集中到少数科技巨头和亿万富翁手里。没有约束，AI 更可能替代劳动、压低工资、制造深伪信息、扩大监控，并消耗社区的电力和水资源。

政策载体是 AI Data Center Moratorium Act——参议院 S.4214，Sanders 于 2026 年 3 月 25 日提出；众议院对应法案 H.R.9442，Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez 于 6 月 24 日提出。值得越过新闻稿去读条款，因为实际内容比报道里的既更宽，也更带条件。

- 触发门槛是 **20 兆瓦**用电需求，加上液冷、机柜功率 20kW 以上这些技术条件。20 兆瓦是中型设施的量级，不只是巨头园区。
- 它覆盖**既有数据中心的扩容**，不只是新建。这是影响最直接的一条，也是各种摘要最常漏掉的一条——包括我自己这篇文章的早前版本。
- 暂停在**"强有力的国家级保护到位"**时解除。没有 sunset 日期，没有量化门槛，也没有指定哪个机构来认定条件已经达成。
- 项目必须证明**不会推高居民电费**，并且必须取得**地方批准**。法案还包含**工会用工和 prevailing wage 要求**，以及**禁止政府补贴**数据中心的建设与运营。
- 另有一部分禁止**向缺乏同等保护的国家出口 AI 算力基础设施**。

其中有几条我直接支持。真实成本电价、社区批准权、prevailing wage，无论是否暂停都是好政策，作为独立法案我会投赞成票。我的反对比"这部法案不好"要窄得多，只针对一个设计选择：一个没有明确解除条件的开放式暂停。

## Point 1: A Pause Freezes The Map It Objects To
## Point 1: 暂停会冻结它自己反对的那张地图

**English:**

Sanders is worried about concentrated power. The worry is valid. A pause distributes nothing — it fixes the current distribution in place, indefinitely, because nothing in the bill says when it ends.

**Pause:** Firms that must build or expand their own capacity get blocked first. That is AI startups training their own models, second-tier cloud providers, and any company whose growth requires new interconnection. Firms that already own compute, cloud platforms, capital, and lobbying capacity can wait. Waiting is cheap when you are already inside the wall.

**Build:** Capacity that keeps growing is capacity that keeps getting cheaper per unit of work, and cheap capability is what puts tools in the hands of people who cannot fund their own.

I have to be careful with the second half of that argument, because the version I used to make was too convenient. I previously wrote that a pause would squeeze out open-source communities, independent developers, and educators. That claim requires three steps: a pause slows supply growth, slower supply growth raises inference prices, and higher inference prices push out the smallest users. Only the first step is solid, and it affects new capacity rather than existing capacity. Token prices have fallen steeply for three years, driven mostly by model efficiency and chip generations rather than by data center count. And if compute genuinely tightened, the first cuts would likely land on marginal internal training runs, not on the consumer subscriptions that anchor these companies' revenue. The squeeze might run the opposite direction from the one I claimed.

So the honest version of the claim is narrower: an open-ended pause hurts the firms that need to build, and those firms are the only plausible source of competition for the incumbents. That is a smaller argument than the one I was making. It is the one I can defend.

**中文：**

Sanders 的核心担心是权力集中。这个担心成立。但暂停不分散任何东西——它把当前分布原样固定住，而且是无限期地，因为法案里没有一句话说它什么时候结束。

**暂停：** 必须自建或扩容的公司先被卡住。这些是自己训练模型的 AI 创业公司、二线云厂商，以及任何增长需要新增电网接入的企业。已经拥有算力、云平台、资本和游说能力的公司可以等。当你已经在墙里面，等待很便宜。

**建造：** 持续增长的产能，意味着单位工作的成本持续下降；而便宜的能力，正是让工具进入那些无力自建的人手里的前提。

第二半我必须说得小心，因为我以前用的版本对自己太方便了。我之前写过：暂停会把开源社区、独立开发者和教育者挤出去。这个说法需要三跳——暂停放慢供给增长，供给增长放慢推高推理价格，推理价格上涨挤出最小的用户。只有第一跳站得住，而且它影响的是新增产能，不是存量。过去三年 token 价格大幅下跌，主因是模型效率和芯片代际，不是数据中心数量。而且如果算力真的紧张，先被砍的多半是边际收益最低的内部训练 run，不是撑起这些公司现金流的消费级订阅。挤出的方向，可能和我说的正好相反。

所以诚实的版本要窄得多：开放式暂停伤害的是那些必须建设的公司，而这些公司是巨头唯一可能的竞争来源。这比我原来的主张小。但这是我守得住的那个。

## Point 2: Capability Is Not Leverage
## Point 2: 能力不等于议价权

**English:**

In Sanders's story, ordinary people appear in a passive position: workers replaced by AI, voters manipulated by algorithms, data sources harvested by platforms. All of that is real. It is also only half the story.

**Before:** Understanding a contract required a lawyer. Reading financial statements required accounting training. Automating a workflow required programming. These capabilities sat behind education, professional networks, and fees most people could not pay.

**After:** An ordinary person can turn AI into a tutor, opposing counsel, research assistant, coding partner, negotiation coach, and fact checker. The cost of that kind of literacy has dropped by orders of magnitude.

Here is where I have to correct my own argument again, because the obvious objection is correct. Capability is not the same thing as power, and treating them as synonyms is the standard mistake technologists make about labor. A warehouse worker who uses AI to raise his own throughput by twenty percent has not gained leverage. He has handed his employer a productivity gain, and who keeps it is settled by his contract, not by his skill. Capability without an institution to capture it is consumer surplus. Consumer surplus is a real good, but it is not a redistribution of power, and it is not by itself a reason to oppose constraints on capital.

Capability converts into leverage only when something can hold it: a union that can bargain over the gain, a legal aid organization that can bring the case, a regulator that can act on the evidence. So the claim I will defend is conditional rather than triumphant. Widely available capability raises the ceiling on what those institutions can do. It does not build them, and it does not substitute for them.

That conditional still matters, because the floor moves too. A public that cannot use AI can only be organized against. A public that can use AI has some chance of auditing it and organizing with it.

**中文：**

Sanders 的叙事里，普通人站在被动位置：被 AI 替代的工人，被算法操纵的选民，被平台收割的数据源。这些都真实。但只是故事的一半。

**之前：** 想理解合同，要找律师。想看懂财报，要懂会计。想搭自动化，要会编程。这些能力被教育背景、职业网络和大多数人付不起的费用锁在门后。

**之后：** 一个普通人可以把 AI 变成私人导师、对方律师、研究助理、代码搭档、谈判教练、事实核查员。这类识读能力的获取成本，已经下降了几个数量级。

这里我要再次修正自己，因为最明显的那个反驳是对的。能力和权力不是一回事，把它们当同义词是技术人谈论劳动关系时的标准错误。一个仓库工人用 AI 把自己的效率提高两成，他并没有获得议价权，他只是把一份生产率收益交给了雇主；这份收益归谁，由他的合同决定，不由他的技能决定。没有机构承接的能力，是消费者剩余。消费者剩余是真实的好东西，但它不是权力再分配，本身也不构成反对约束资本的理由。

能力要变成议价权，得有东西接得住：一个能就这份收益谈判的工会，一个能把案子打出来的法律援助机构，一个能依据证据行动的监管者。所以我要守的主张是有条件的，不是凯旋式的——普遍可得的能力抬高了这些机构的能力上限。它不负责把这些机构建起来，也不能替代它们。

这个有条件的版本仍然重要，因为下限同时也在移动。一个不会使用 AI 的公众，只能成为被组织的对象。一个会使用 AI 的公众，才有机会审计它，并借助它把自己组织起来。

## Point 3: The Counter-Tools, And Who Pays For Them
## Point 3: 反击工具，以及谁来付钱

**English:**

Big companies will use AI to maximize profit. Ordinary people need AI to sharpen bargaining, detection, and organizing power. People-first AI should not be a gentler chatbot. It should be sharper.

A contract reviewer that flags dangerous non-compete terms. A price detector that checks whether a platform is personalizing prices against you. A tenant rights assistant that tells renters whether a landlord's terms are legal. A fact-checking tool that traces the holes in political ads and corporate PR. An analysis tool that turns a union's scattered member complaints into structured evidence.

Now the hard question, and it is the strongest objection to this entire post: look at the users of those six tools. Workers signing non-competes, consumers being price-targeted, renters, students, unions. That is a list of people who cannot pay. None of these tools has a business model, and I have already argued that large companies follow their incentives away from exactly this work. So who builds them?

I do not have a complete answer. Here is the incomplete one I am actually betting on. The cost of building this class of tool is falling faster than the funding gap is growing, and it is falling because of open models and open evaluation frameworks. A legal aid clinic cannot commission a frontier lab. It can fine-tune an open model on its own case history if the open models stay within range of the frontier and if the tooling to audit them is public. That is the bet. It has a clear failure condition: if open-weight models fall decisively behind and stay behind, this argument collapses and the funding problem becomes the whole problem.

Which is why my alternative is not a permitting regime. It is a technical one: open, auditable safety frameworks that anyone can run, inspect, and contest — rather than a government approval gate that only well-lawyered incumbents can reliably clear. A gate favors whoever can afford to wait at it. A published standard favors whoever can meet it.

**中文：**

大公司会用 AI 最大化利润。普通人需要用 AI 提高谈判能力、识别能力和组织能力。People-first AI 不该只是更温柔的聊天机器人，它应该更锋利。

合同审阅器，指出竞业协议里的危险条款。价格侦测器，发现平台有没有对你做个性化加价。租客权益助手，帮租户判断房东条款是否合法。事实核查工具，追踪政治广告和企业公关稿里的漏洞。工会分析工具，把分散的会员投诉变成结构化证据。

现在是最难的问题，也是对这整篇文章最强的反驳：看看这六个工具的用户——签竞业协议的员工、被个性化加价的消费者、租客、学生、工会。这是一份付不起钱的人的名单。它们没有一个有商业模式，而我前面已经论证过大公司的激励恰好背离这类工作。那么谁来建？

我没有完整答案。下面是我实际押注的那个不完整答案：建造这类工具的成本，下降得比资金缺口扩大得更快，而它下降的原因是开放模型和开放的评测框架。一家法律援助机构没法向前沿实验室下订单，但如果开放模型和前沿差距保持在可用范围内、并且审计工具是公开的，它可以用自己的案件史微调一个开放模型。这就是那个赌注。它有明确的失败条件：如果开放权重模型被决定性地甩开并且一直追不上，这个论证就垮了，资金问题会变成全部问题。

也正因如此，我的替代方案不是许可制，而是技术方案：开放、可审计、任何人都能运行、检查和质疑的安全框架——而不是一道只有请得起律师的在位者才能稳定通过的政府审批闸门。闸门有利于耗得起的人，公开标准有利于达得到的人。

## The Strongest Case For The Pause
## 支持暂停的最强理由

**English:**

The best argument for a moratorium is not the environmental one, though that one is real: data centers consume electricity, water, land, tax incentives, and grid capacity, and if a community bears those costs while the gains flow to a few platforms, the public has a legitimate objection.

The best argument is that nobody serious thinks the pause is the endpoint. It is leverage. A moratorium manufactures scarcity in permitting, and permitting scarcity is what you trade for safeguards you could not otherwise pass. Environmental law has worked this way for fifty years. Read that way, the bill is not a wall; it is a negotiating position, and the union labor, community approval, and true-cost provisions are what it intends to buy.

That argument lands, and I have to concede something to it. Every item on my own list below — externality pricing, mandatory audits, public capacity — needs legislative leverage to pass, and I have not identified where that leverage comes from if not from something like this bill. That is a real hole in my position, and I would rather name it than paper over it.

My disagreement is about the instrument, not the goal. A permitting chokepoint buys safeguards by making capacity scarce, and scarcity is exactly what an incumbent converts into rent. I would rather buy the same safeguards with binding technical standards — published, auditable, enforceable against anyone regardless of size — because a standard does not create a queue that the largest player is best positioned to survive.

And the forced choice, since a reader is entitled to ask it: if I could not have the standards, would I take the pause over the status quo? No. I would take the status quo, and I want to flag how much of that answer is my prior rather than my analysis. I believe most problems are problems of insufficient development. Someone who does not share that belief, and who weighs irreversible harm more heavily than foregone capability, can accept every fact in this post and land on the opposite answer. I do not think they are being unreasonable.

**中文：**

支持暂停的最好理由不是环境理由，尽管环境理由是真实的：数据中心消耗电力、水、土地、税收优惠和电网容量；如果社区承担这些成本而收益流向少数平台，公众当然有权反对。

最好的理由是：真正懂行的人没有谁认为暂停是终局状态。它是筹码。暂停在许可环节人为制造稀缺，而许可稀缺正是你用来交换那些原本通不过的保护条款的东西。环境法这么运作了五十年。这样读，这部法案就不是一堵墙，而是一个谈判位置；工会用工、社区批准、真实成本这些条款，正是它打算换回来的东西。

这个论点成立，我得让出一些东西。我下面自己那份清单上的每一项——外部性定价、强制审计、公共算力——都需要立法杠杆才能通过，而如果不靠类似这部法案的东西，我没能指出这个杠杆从哪来。这是我立场上的一个真实缺口，我宁愿把它说出来，也不想糊过去。

我的分歧在工具，不在目标。许可闸门是靠制造产能稀缺来换取保护，而稀缺恰恰是在位者最擅长转化成租金的东西。我更愿意用有约束力的技术标准去换同样的保护——公开、可审计、对任何规模的主体一体执行——因为标准不会制造一条队伍，而排队这件事，最大的玩家最耗得起。

还有那个被逼到墙角的选择题，读者有权问：如果拿不到标准，我会选暂停还是现状？我选现状。而我要标明，这个答案里有多少来自我的先验，而不是我的分析。我相信大部分问题都是发展不充足的问题。一个不共享这个信念、并且把不可逆的伤害看得比放弃的能力更重的人，可以接受这篇文章里的每一个事实，然后落到相反的答案上。我不认为那样不讲道理。

## What Regulation Should Do Instead
## 监管应该改做什么

**English:**

The useful distinction is between regulating **uses** and regulating **infrastructure**. Most of the harm Sanders names comes from uses. Most of what the moratorium blocks is infrastructure. That mismatch is my whole objection, compressed into one line.

So: slow harmful uses, price externalities, force auditability, fund public capability.

- Charge data centers the true electricity, water, and grid-upgrade cost, and fight that fight at the state utility commissions, where special rate classes quietly shift costs onto residential ratepayers. That is where this is actually decided, not in Congress.
- Require independent audit access for high-risk systems, with published methods rather than private attestations.
- Strengthen data rights and privacy protection.
- Use antitrust against vertical bundling across chips, cloud, models, and distribution.
- Fund public compute for schools, libraries, community colleges, unions, and local governments — and note honestly that the bill's ban on subsidies for data center operation may cut against this, which is a conflict I would want resolved in the bill's favor on subsidies to firms and against it on capacity for institutions.
- Require firms capturing large AI productivity gains to fund retraining and transitions.
- Support open models, open evaluation, and local deployment, because that is the mechanism the rest of my argument depends on.

This path is harder than a pause. The goal is not that AI arrives more slowly. It is that AI capability does not flow only to capital.

**中文：**

有用的分野是：管**用途**，还是管**基础设施**。Sanders 点名的伤害，大部分来自用途。暂停令挡住的，大部分是基础设施。我的全部反对，压缩成这一句就够了。

所以：放慢有害用途，为外部性定价，强制可审计，资助公共能力。

- 让数据中心承担真实的电力、水和电网升级成本，而且这一仗要在各州公用事业委员会打——special rate class 正是在那里悄悄把成本转嫁给居民用户的。真正的决策在那里，不在国会。
- 要求高风险系统开放独立审计通道，方法必须公开，而不是私下出具的合规声明。
- 强化数据权利和隐私保护。
- 用反垄断限制芯片、云、模型、分发渠道之间的纵向捆绑。
- 为学校、图书馆、社区学院、工会和地方政府提供公共算力——同时诚实地指出，法案里禁止补贴数据中心运营的条款可能和这一条冲突。这个冲突我希望这样解决：对企业的补贴照禁，对公共机构的算力照给。
- 要求因 AI 获得重大生产率收益的企业承担再培训和转岗成本。
- 支持开放模型、开放评测和本地部署——因为我这篇文章其余部分的论证，全都依赖这个机制。

这条路比暂停更难。目标不是让 AI 慢一点到来，而是不让 AI 的能力只流向资本。

## The Test Is Who Ends Up Holding It
## 检验标准：最后谁拿在手里

**English:**

Sanders's warning is worth hearing. Technology does not automatically side with ordinary people, and a tool that arrives without institutions to hold it will be held by whoever already holds everything else.

But a construction pause is not neutral either. In a market where the giants lead and infrastructure is the scarce input, an indefinite pause converts scarcity into rent for the people who already have capacity. The real question is not whether to regulate AI. It is whether ordinary people end up holding any of it.

I choose building, with the interest and the prior I disclosed at the top.

That choice should be checkable, so here is what would show me wrong. By December 2027: at least three major open-weight model families still shipping weights under commercially usable licenses and within roughly a generation of the frontier; federal appropriations for public compute exceeding $500 million in a single enacted bill; and at least one worker-facing AI tool — contract review, wage claims, tenant rights — past a million monthly users. If all three fail, then cheap capability did not reach the people I said it would reach, the funding hole in Point 3 was the whole story, and the pause camp was right about how scarcity actually moves.

Check me in December 2027.

**中文：**

Sanders 的警告值得听。技术不会自动站在普通人这边；一件没有机构去承接的工具，最后会落到本来就握有一切的人手里。

但暂停建设同样不是中立的。在一个巨头领先、基础设施是稀缺投入的市场里，无限期暂停会把稀缺转化成租金，交给已经拥有产能的人。真正的问题不是要不要监管 AI，而是普通人最后能不能拿到一点。

我选择建造——带着开头披露的那份利益和那份先验。

这个选择应该可以被检验，所以我写下什么情况说明我错了。到 2027 年 12 月：至少三个主要的开放权重模型家族仍在以可商用许可发布权重，且与前沿的差距大致保持在一个代际以内；单一已通过法案中的联邦公共算力拨款超过五亿美元；以及至少一个面向劳动者的 AI 工具——合同审阅、欠薪申诉、租客权益——月活跃用户超过一百万。如果这三条全部落空，那就说明便宜的能力没有到达我说的那些人手里，Point 3 里那个资金缺口才是故事的全部，而暂停一方对稀缺如何流动的判断是对的。

2027 年 12 月来查我。
